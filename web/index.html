    How to read this

    A campaign

    One dock and one to five identical Scouts. Each sortie, one Scout drives down from the dock into a shadowed crater, surveys lines with its neutron sensor, and drives home to charge. A reading above the ice threshold is a find. Another Scout later searches the area round it: a matching reading there confirms it, and a confirmed find can be drilled. Craters a Scout sees that are not on its map are merged into the dock's shared crater map after each sortie. A run is one campaign, played sortie by sortie: pick a sortie on the left and press play.

    First person and lidar

    Sensor frames are rendered live in the simulation and not saved. The first-person box is drawn here from the 3D ground at the Scout's camera pose (2.5 m up, 15° down, 60° wide, lamp only), with what its pipeline logged at that step on top: the crater-rim points it navigated by (squares; hollow when no mapped crater matched), the route it planned, and the hazard cells its lidar marked. The lidar box shows the logged scan: top-down, forward up, rings every 5 m; first person, the same 120° wedge drawn from the 3D ground with the logged hazards coloured in.

    • GreyGround the lidar judged safe
    • AmberCrater floor; the planner avoids it
    • RedSteeper than 15°: no-go

    Navigation

    In a dark crater a Scout finds its position from the shadows its own lamp casts at crater rims, after JPL's ShadowNav (Cauligi et al. 2023, arXiv:2301.04630): a particle filter on wheel odometry and the rims it matches to its crater map. The Scout cards show where each Scout thinks it is against where it is.
